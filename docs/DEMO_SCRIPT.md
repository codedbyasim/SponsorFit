# SponsorFit demo script

Two and a half minutes. Record at 1920×1080, browser zoom 100%, dark page. Start on the landing hero with no result yet. Do not show `.env` files, terminal keys, or network headers.

If the Qloo key is connected, the badge must read **Live Qloo data**. If it is not, use the sample and say the words “demo data” out loud once. Do not present sample affinities as measurements.

## Scene 1 — The desk (0:00–0:20)

On screen: hero, “The room already has a favorite brand.”, empty trace, example chips.

Voiceover:

“Sponsorship decks still start with the logo on the jersey. SponsorFit starts with the room. You give it a team, a creator, an artist, or an event. It asks Qloo which brands that audience already over-indexes on, scores the fit in the open, and writes the pitch from those signals. Nothing to sign up for. Chelsea is one click.”

Action: hover the Chelsea FC chip, then click it.

## Scene 2 — The agent (0:20–0:50)

On screen: the trace filling in. Cue 01 resolve, 03 brand affinities, 04 audience, 06 Fit Score. Leave the compare field empty for this pass.

Voiceover:

“The trace is the agent, not a spinner. It resolves Chelsea in search, pulls brand insights with explainability turned on, then reads demographics and taste tags. Artists, films, and cities come back as color. Fit Score is ours: sixty percent Qloo affinity, twenty-five popularity, fifteen how closely the brand’s age indexes match the club’s. If Qloo didn’t return a number, that term is dropped. We do not fill it in.”

Action: when the board lands, point at the badge, then at Nike’s Fit bar and the formula under the table.

## Scene 3 — The board (0:50–1:25)

On screen: sponsor table, why chips, factor breakdown. Scroll just enough to show the audience panel and the London map.

Voiceover:

“Each row is a brand Qloo returned for this audience. The why chip is the explainability signal, or an honest gap if it wasn’t returned. Age indexes sit above or below Qloo’s baseline. They describe the group, not a person. The map is heatmap cells inside the top returned city — London for this sample — for the lead brand. Those are pockets in one city. We don’t invent a neighborhood name from a coordinate.”

Action: click the second brand so the factor weights change if the formula differs. Point at a taste tag and a destination.

## Scene 4 — Chelsea versus the Dodgers (1:25–2:05)

Action: click **Chelsea vs Dodgers**. Let the trace hit cue 08. Scroll to Compare, then to the first pitch card.

Voiceover:

“Same desk, two rooms. Nike, American Express, Samsung, and Uber show up for both. Guinness and EA Sports stay with Chelsea. Sony, Toyota, and Modelo stay with the Dodgers. That overlap is our comparison of the two brand lists. Next to it, Qloo’s compare endpoint contributes tags, and we don’t pretend those tags are the same measurement. The pitch for the lead brand cites affinity, popularity, the age index, the taste tag, and London. The target city is a returned destination. Copy the brief, or download the one-page HTML.”

Action: click Copy brief. Flash the button state. Do not open a file with secrets.

## Scene 5 — Close (2:05–2:25)

On screen: privacy line at the bottom, badge still visible.

Voiceover:

“No personal data goes to Qloo. No health, politics, religion, or finances. Affinity is not a prediction about a fan, and Fit Score is not a price. It’s the first page of a sponsorship meeting — Chelsea, the Dodgers, or whoever you type once the live key is on.”

## If you are on live data

Swap the sample-specific brand names for whoever actually ranks. Keep every sentence tied to a number on screen. If explainability is empty, say “Qloo didn’t return explainability on this call” and move to affinity. If the compare tags arrive in one unlabeled list, say that, and lean on the shared-brand table.
